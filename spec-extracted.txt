1. Project Overview
The AI Resume Screening Platform is a recruitment management system that helps authorized 
recruitment teams review applications consistently and efficiently. Recruiters create or upload a job 
description, convert it into an approved set of job requirements, receive candidate resumes, and use 
artificial intelligence to compare each application against those requirements. The platform produces 
an evidence-based match assessment, explains how each result was reached, and supports the 
human recruitment team in deciding which candidates should move forward.
The platform is not intended to make autonomous employment decisions. Its purpose is to organize 
information, identify relevant evidence in resumes, highlight possible matches and gaps, and reduce 
repetitive manual review. Every shortlist, rejection, interview decision, and final recruitment 
outcome must remain under authorized human control and must be recorded with a clear reason 
and audit history.
The complete system must cover the recruitment workflow from job setup and resume submission 
through screening, recruiter review, candidate management, interview scheduling, feedback 
collection, communications, analytics, privacy controls, and final handover. The core deliverable 
must be a usable business application rather than a demonstration that only uploads a resume and 
returns a score.

2. Business Problem and Project Rationale
Recruitment teams frequently receive more applications than they can review quickly. Manual 
screening can become inconsistent, difficult to audit, and heavily dependent on how individual 
reviewers interpret a job description. Important candidates may be overlooked, while weak matches 
may advance because their resumes use familiar wording. Recruiters also lose time moving 
candidate information between email, spreadsheets, calendars, and disconnected interview notes.
This project addresses those challenges through one controlled recruitment workspace. Job 
requirements are structured and approved before screening begins; resumes are processed into 
reviewable candidate profiles; AI-generated assessments are supported by evidence from the 
application; and all decisions remain traceable to the responsible recruiter or hiring manager. The 
system must improve efficiency without replacing professional judgement, introducing prohibited 
discrimination, or hiding the reasons behind a recommendation.
3. Project Objectives
1. Build a secure, role-based recruitment platform that supports job setup, resume intake, AI-assisted 
screening, candidate management, interview scheduling, feedback, communications, and analytics.
2. Convert each job description into a structured and supervisor-approved screening rubric containing 
required qualifications, preferred qualifications, skills, experience, education, and configurable 
weighting rules.
3. Generate transparent candidate assessments that show the evidence, gaps, uncertainty, and scoring 
logic used for each recommendation.
4. Keep all employment decisions under authorized human review and provide a complete audit trail of 
automated assessments and human actions.
5. Protect candidate information through appropriate access control, consent, retention, secure file 
handling, and data-minimization practices.
6. Apply sound software engineering practices including modular design, validation, testing, error 
handling, documentation, deployment, monitoring, and reproducible configuration.
7. Deliver a practical system that can be configured for a small or medium-sized organization and safely 
extended after the core workflow is stable.
4. Scope and Completion Standard
4.1 Core Scope
 Secure user authentication, organization separation, role-based permissions, and auditable 
administrative actions.
 Job requisition and job-description management, including screening criteria, required and preferred 
qualifications, scoring weights, approval status, and version history.
 Candidate profiles, application records, resume uploads, document validation, text extraction, 
structured data review, duplicate detection, and recruitment history.
 AI-assisted skill matching, qualification assessment, candidate ranking, evidence display, gap 
identification, uncertainty handling, and human review controls.
 Recruiter workspace for searching, filtering, comparing, shortlisting, rejecting, tagging, commenting, 
assigning, and moving candidates through configurable recruitment stages.
 Interview scheduling, interviewer assignment, invitations, rescheduling, cancellation, feedback forms, 
scorecards, and recommendation capture.

 Recruiter and management dashboards covering pipeline status, screening activity, stage conversion, 
interview progress, processing time, and system quality indicators.
 Candidate and staff notifications, communication templates, delivery status, consent records, retention

4.2 Minimum Completion Standard
The project is considered functionally complete when an authorized recruiter can create an 
approved job and screening rubric, receive multiple candidate applications, review extracted resume 
information, run an explainable AI-assisted comparison, inspect evidence for every criterion, make 
and record a human screening decision, schedule interviews, collect interviewer feedback, move 
candidates through the recruitment pipeline, and view accurate dashboard information. The 
application must be deployed to a staging environment, tested against the acceptance requirements, 
and documented so another authorized team member can operate it.
4.3 Excluded from the Core Version
 Fully autonomous rejection, shortlisting, hiring, or offer decisions without authorized human 
approval.
 Face analysis, voice analysis, emotion detection, personality inference, lie detection, or any evaluation 
based on appearance or biometric characteristics.
 Scraping private social-media profiles or using unrelated personal information to evaluate 
employability.
 Inferring race, ethnicity, religion, health status, disability, pregnancy, political opinion, sexual 
orientation, or other sensitive personal characteristics.
 Background checks, payroll, employee onboarding, digital contracts, and full human-resource 
management functions.
 Large external job-board, calendar, assessment-provider, or enterprise HR integrations unless 
separately approved after the core platform is stable.

6. Functional Requirements
Every requirement below must be implemented as a testable behavior. Permissions must be enforced 
in both the user interface and the underlying services; hiding an action in the interface is not 
sufficient authorization.
6.1 Authentication, Organization and Access Control
 Authorized staff must be able to sign in securely; credentials must never be stored in plain text.
 The system must support secure session handling, logout, session expiry, password reset, and account 
activation or suspension.
 Role-based authorization must be enforced on every protected operation, report, file, and service 
endpoint.
 Administrators must be able to create users, assign roles, limit users to permitted jobs or departments, 
and revoke access immediately.
 Data belonging to one organization must not be visible to another organization when the platform 
supports more than one organization.
 Sensitive account, permission, configuration, and data-access actions must be written to an immutable 
or tamper-evident audit history.
6.2 Job Requisition and Screening Rubric Management
 Recruiters or managers must be able to create, edit, duplicate, archive, and close job requisitions with 
title, department, location, employment type, description, responsibilities, and application dates.
 A job description must be converted into structured screening criteria before candidate ranking is 
permitted.
 Each criterion must identify its category, description, whether it is required or preferred, its weight, 
acceptable evidence, and any minimum threshold.
 Criteria must support skills, experience, education, certifications, language, work authorization where 
legally appropriate, location constraints, and other job-related requirements approved by the 
organization.
 The complete weighting must be validated so the calculation is understandable and internally 
consistent.
 The rubric must have draft, review, approved, and archived states. Only an approved version may be 
used for official screening.

 Changes to an approved rubric must create a new version, identify the editor and reason, and must 
not silently alter previous screening results.
 The system must provide a readable preview showing how the rubric will evaluate candidates before 
screening begins.
6.3 Candidate and Application Management
 Authorized users must be able to create, view, update, search, filter, tag, assign, archive, and export 
candidate and application records according to their permissions.
 A candidate may apply to more than one job, but each application must retain its own status, 
screening history, communications, interview records, and decision trail.
 The system must maintain contact information, source, application date, current stage, assigned 
recruiter, notes, tags, consent status, and permitted recruitment history.
 Potential duplicate candidates must be identified using controlled matching rules and presented for 
human review before records are merged.
 Recruiters must be able to add internal notes that are never shown to candidates and public notes or 
status messages where appropriate.
 Candidate stage changes must be recorded with actor, timestamp, previous stage, new stage, and 
reason where required.
 Bulk actions must require confirmation and must not bypass authorization, audit, or required reason 
capture.
6.4 Resume Upload, Validation and Processing
 Candidates and authorized staff must be able to upload resumes in approved document formats within 
configured size limits.
 The system must validate file type and size, reject unsafe or unsupported files, and store the original 
document securely with a unique reference.
 Uploaded documents must be checked for malicious content using an approved security process 
before they are made available to staff or processing services.
 The platform must extract readable text and identify structured information such as skills, employers, 
job titles, dates, education, certifications, projects, and languages where present.
 The original resume, extracted text, and normalized profile must remain linked so recruiters can 
verify automated extraction against the source document.
 Low-confidence or missing extraction results must be clearly marked for manual correction rather 
than silently guessed.
 Authorized recruiters must be able to correct extracted information without modifying the original 
document, and every correction must be auditable.
 A processing status must show whether a resume is queued, processing, completed, needs review, or 
failed, with safe retry behavior and an understandable error message.
6.5 AI-Assisted Candidate Matching and Ranking
 The screening process must compare each application only against the approved rubric for the 
selected job.
 The system must calculate criterion-level results and an overall assessment using the approved 
weighting and threshold rules.
 Each criterion result must show supporting evidence from the resume or application, the source 
location, the interpretation, the score contribution, and any uncertainty.
 Required criteria that are not evidenced must be displayed as missing or unverified; the system must 
distinguish absence of evidence from confirmed absence of a qualification.

 The system must identify matched skills, related or transferable skills, missing requirements, and 
ambiguous information that requires human review.
 Candidate ranking must be reproducible for the same job version, resume version, configuration, and 
AI version, or the system must explain why results changed.
 The platform must store the screening time, rubric version, resume version, system or model version, 
configuration, criterion outputs, overall result, and processing errors.
 Recruiters must be able to rerun screening after an approved change while retaining earlier results 
for comparison and audit.
 The system must not automatically reject or advance a candidate solely because of the AI result; a 
permitted human user must confirm the decision.
 A recruiter must be able to override a recommendation, but the final action must record the human 
reason and must not overwrite the original AI assessment.
6.6 Skill and Qualification Matching
 The platform must normalize common skill variations and abbreviations while preserving the 
wording found in the candidate document.
 Exact, related, transferable, and missing skills must be presented separately so recruiters can 
understand the type of match.
 Experience calculations must account for overlapping dates, incomplete dates, internships, part-time 
work, and gaps without inventing precision that is not supported by the resume.
 Education and certification matching must distinguish field of study, level, institution, completion 
status, validity dates where relevant, and equivalent qualifications where the approved rubric allows 
them.
 A user must be able to inspect the evidence behind every matched skill or qualification and correct the 
structured record when extraction is wrong.
 Job-related requirements must be evaluated consistently across all applicants to the same approved 
job version.
6.7 Human Review, Search, Comparison and Decisions
 Recruiters must have a work queue showing new, processing, review-required, screened, shortlisted, 
rejected, interview, offer, hired, and withdrawn applications according to the configured workflow.
 Users must be able to search and filter by job, stage, recruiter, score range, required-criterion status, 
skills, experience, education, application date, tags, and processing status.
 Candidate comparison must show the same approved criteria in the same order and must display 
evidence and uncertainty, not only total scores.
 Recruiters must be able to shortlist, reject, hold, advance, withdraw, or return an application to 
review, subject to permission and required reason rules.
 The system must support configurable decision reasons and free-text justification while preventing 
discriminatory or irrelevant decision categories.
 A final screening action must show the responsible human reviewer and must never be represented as 
an autonomous AI decision.
 Hiring managers must only view candidates and information associated with jobs assigned to them.
6.8 Interview Scheduling and Evaluation
 Authorized users must be able to create interview stages, select candidates, assign interviewers, 
propose or confirm date and time, specify timezone, location or meeting details, and add instructions.
 The system must detect scheduling conflicts within information available to it and require 
confirmation before double-booking an interviewer or candidate.

 Candidates and interviewers must receive approved invitations and updates for scheduling, 
rescheduling, or cancellation.
 Interview records must show status, participants, schedule, communication history, and permitted 
preparation materials.
 Interviewers must complete structured scorecards based on approved job-related criteria and may 
add evidence-based comments.
 Interview feedback must remain private from candidates unless an authorized user intentionally

 The platform must prevent one interviewer from viewing another interviewer's submitted feedback

6.9 Communications and Notifications
 The system must support approved templates for application acknowledgement, status updates, 
interview invitations, rescheduling, rejection, withdrawal confirmation, and other recruitment 
messages.
 Authorized users must be able to preview and edit permitted message fields before sending.
 Messages must record recipient, sender or system actor, template version, time, delivery status, and 
failure information.
 Failed notifications must be retried safely where appropriate and displayed to authorized users for 
follow-up.
 Internal notes, AI prompts, private reviewer comments, and other confidential information must 
never be inserted into candidate communications.
 Candidates must not be informed that a final employment decision was made solely by AI.
6.10 Recruiter Dashboard, Analytics and Reports
 The role-based dashboard must show active jobs, application volume, resumes awaiting processing, 
applications requiring review, stage distribution, upcoming interviews, and recent recruitment 
activity.
 Managers must be able to view time-to-screen, time-in-stage, stage conversion, interviewer 
completion, processing failure, and recruiter workload metrics for permitted jobs and periods.
 Users must be able to filter analytics by date range, job, department, recruiter, stage, and source where 
permitted.
 Screening quality indicators must include low-confidence extraction, manual corrections, 
recommendation overrides, failed processing, and disagreement between AI assessment and final 
human decision.
 Reports must be exportable in common business formats while respecting access permissions and 
privacy settings.
 Metric definitions must be documented and the same filters must produce consistent totals across 
dashboards and exports.
 Sensitive demographic analytics must not be included in the core version unless the organization has 
an approved legal, ethical, and access-control basis for collecting and using the data.
6.11 Candidate Privacy, Consent, Retention and Rights
 The candidate-facing process must present an understandable privacy notice and record the version 
accepted, timestamp, and relevant consent choices where required.
 The system must collect only information necessary for the recruitment purpose and must not require 
unrelated sensitive personal data.

 Authorized users must be able to configure retention periods by application status and apply archive, 
anonymization, or deletion workflows subject to organizational policy and applicable obligations.
 Candidate withdrawal, correction, access, or deletion requests must be tracked and routed to 
authorized staff for review and completion.
 Deleting or anonymizing a candidate must address resumes, extracted text, AI screening records,

 Downloaded resumes and exported reports must be treated as controlled personal data and must be

6.12 Administration, Configuration and Audit
 Authorized administrators must be able to configure recruitment stages, decision reasons, job

branding.
 Configuration changes that can affect screening or decisions must record the old value, new value, 
actor, time, and reason.
 The audit log must support search by user, candidate, application, job, action type, and date range 
without allowing ordinary users to alter the history.
 Administrators must be able to view processing health, failed background tasks, notification failures, 
storage status, and integration status without exposing secrets.
 The system must support safe sample data and demonstration accounts that do not contain real 
candidate information.
 Critical actions such as changing a rubric, deleting candidate information, changing permissions, or 
performing a bulk decision must require confirmation.
7. Responsible AI and Recruitment Governance Requirements
Principle Mandatory Requirement
Human Decision Authority
AI output must be decision support only. Authorized 
humans remain accountable for screening, shortlisting, 
interview, rejection, offer, and hiring decisions.
Job Relevance
The system must evaluate only approved, job-related 
criteria and must not use personal information that is 
irrelevant to the work.
Protected and Sensitive Attributes
The platform must not score, rank, or infer candidates using 
protected or sensitive characteristics. Such information 
must not be passed to the screening process unless an 
approved, lawful, and necessary exception is documented.
Proxy Risk
Design and testing must consider indirect proxies such as 
names, photographs, addresses, graduation years, and other 
information that may reveal or approximate sensitive 
characteristics.
Explainability
Every recommendation must show criterion-level reasoning 
and resume evidence in language a recruiter can 
understand and challenge.

Principle Mandatory Requirement
Uncertainty
The system must clearly identify ambiguous, missing, low-

Consistency

No Hidden Learning
Candidate resumes, recruiter decisions, and interview

Prompt and Document Safety
Resume content must be treated as untrusted input. 
Instructions embedded inside a resume must not be allowed 
to change system rules, reveal confidential information, or 
manipulate scoring.
Monitoring and Review
Authorized users must be able to review errors, overrides, 
complaints, low-confidence cases, and unusual score 
patterns so the system can be corrected without silently 
changing historical decisions.

11. Technology Selection and Design Approval
This specification intentionally does not prescribe programming languages, frameworks, databases, 
AI providers, hosting services, or development tools. Interns must first demonstrate that they 
understand the business problem, recruitment workflow, data sensitivity, responsible-AI obligations, 
security risks, expected scale, and completion standard. Technology choices must follow that 
understanding rather than determine it.
 Before implementation, the intern must prepare a concise technical proposal covering the proposed 
architecture, major components, data model, resume-processing approach, AI-assessment approach, 
access-control model, background processing, testing strategy, deployment approach, monitoring, 
estimated costs, limitations, and important risks.
 The proposal must explain why the selected approach is suitable for maintainability, security, privacy, 
explainability, reliability, available skills, expected workload, and the company's operating 
environment.
 The intern must identify any external service that would receive candidate data, what data it receives, 
where it is processed or stored where known, how credentials are protected, and what fallback exists 
if the service is unavailable.
 The assigned supervisor must approve the technical direction before substantial implementation 
begins.
 A major change to an approved architecture, data flow, AI provider, storage method, security control, 
or deployment approach must be discussed with and approved by the supervisor.
 Where a requirement is unclear, the intern must ask the supervisor rather than selecting a technology 
or changing scope based on an assumption.

14. Testing and Acceptance Requirements
 Unit tests must cover permission decisions, rubric validation, score calculations, status transitions, 
retention rules, interview conflicts, and human-approval requirements.
 Integration tests must cover database changes, organization isolation, file storage and retrieval, 
resume processing, screening persistence, notifications, scheduling, exports, audit events, and 
important failure and retry paths.
 End-to-end tests must cover job approval, candidate application, resume processing, recruiter 
correction, AI-assisted screening, evidence review, human decision, interview scheduling, feedback, 
candidate communication, reporting, and privacy requests.
 The AI evaluation set must include strong matches, weak matches, transferable skills, missing 
evidence, ambiguous dates, overlapping employment, unusual resume layouts, unsupported 
instructions embedded in resumes, and deliberately irrelevant personal details.
 Fairness checks must compare equivalent resumes in which non-job-related identifying details are 
changed. Material score or ranking differences must be investigated, corrected, or clearly documented 
before handover.
 Security testing must include unauthorized record access, cross-organization access, insecure file 
types, oversized files, filename manipulation, malicious document content, secret exposure, rate 
abuse, and permission changes.
 Recruiters must be able to trace every official recommendation to an approved rubric version and 
resume evidence, and every progression decision to an authorized human actor.
 No critical or high-severity security, privacy, data-loss, or discriminatory-behavior defect may remain 
open at handover. Lower-severity limitations must be documented with impact and recommended 
correction.
 A new authorized team member must be able to start, configure, and evaluate the system by following 
the repository documentation and safe sample configuration without undocumented manual steps.

17. Notes for the Intern
 Do not begin by building pages from the short project description. First understand the recruitment 
problem, users, decision points, job criteria, candidate data, responsible-AI boundaries, failure cases, 
privacy risks, and completion standard.
 Submit the required GitHub username promptly and confirm that access to the assigned company- or 
supervisor-owned repository works before project files are created.
 Keep the assigned repository current throughout the project. Work that exists only locally, in a 
personal repository, or in an unapproved copy cannot be treated as properly submitted project work.
 Build one complete vertical slice early: approved job rubric -> safe resume upload -> extracted 
evidence -> criterion assessment -> recruiter review -> recorded human decision. Expand only after 
this path works reliably.
 Do not treat a high AI score as proof that a candidate is suitable, and do not treat missing resume 
evidence as proof that a candidate lacks the qualification. Preserve uncertainty and require 
professional review.
 Design fairness, privacy, access control, auditability, and human oversight from the beginning. These 
controls are difficult to add safely after the application becomes large.
 Use realistic but synthetic or otherwise approved sample candidate information. Never commit 
credentials, private keys, real resumes, confidential recruitment records, or personal data to the 
repository.
 Whenever any requirement, result, risk, or technical decision is not understood, record the question 
and contact the assigned supervisor. Interns must not guess, hide a blocking issue, or silently change 
the approved scope.
